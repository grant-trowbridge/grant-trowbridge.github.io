## SRC

**Cybersecurity Engineer II: July 2022 - June 2026**

After graduation, and lots of applications, I received an offer for a Cybersecurity Engineer position at [Scientific Research Corporation](https://www.scires.com/) in Charleston, South Carolina. What I wasn't expecting was the overwhelming and intricate world of Risk Management Framework (RMF), a system used by the DoW to manage Cybersecurity. If a system was going to connect to a DoW network, it had to have a valid ADD.

My favorite part of RMF was getting hands-on with systems and learning their specific Security Technical Implementation Guides (STIGs). It was a great way to become familiarized with an operating system and sharpen my command line skills. PowerShell became second-nature to me, and I even learned to use DotNET reflection to expand the use of my scripts. I then standardized these scripts through GitLab version control to convey changes more clearly to our end users.

The achievement I am most proud of during my time at SRC was successfully migrating one of my project's system variants to Windows 11 with Ansible. On any legacy project, standardization is hard to implement when you inherit systems, processes, and code from people who are long gone from the project.

Luckily, I had some of the most talented engineers to foster my growth who were always available to answer my questions. I'd like to specifically thank [Donald Haywood](https://www.linkedin.com/in/donald-haywood-6630b2a3/) for believing in me and taking a chance on a new grad. Donald and his **rock-solid** cyber team were always there when I needed to call in the big guns.
